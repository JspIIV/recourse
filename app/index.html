The verdict is the payout.

A client locks a fee. A worker delivers. If the client contests it, the brief, the delivery and the complaint go to one consensus round, and the money follows whichever way it rules. Nobody here confirms the result afterwards.

What makes this different from a verdict on a screen

The ruling moves the fee. Plenty of marketplaces will show you an AI verdict on a dispute. Almost none let it touch the funds, because the moment it does, every weakness in it costs somebody real money. A verdict that changes nothing is a decoration.

One field goes to consensus. RELEASE or REFUND. The amounts are never asked: the contract computes them, because asking a model to divide a fee is asking witnesses to agree on arithmetic the contract can do exactly and for free.

Nobody's money is locked without a way out. If the worker never delivers, the client reclaims the fee after the delivery deadline. If the client never answers a delivery, the worker claims it after the review window. Both are enforced by the chain rather than by anybody's agreement, so a party who walks away cannot freeze somebody else's money.

The deliverable is pinned. A delivery can carry a URI and a digest computed off chain, recorded in the same transaction. The contract cannot fetch it, and a round that tried would time out on this validator set, so it does the one thing it can: it fixes what was handed in, so the thing being argued about cannot be swapped afterwards.

An unreadable round settles nothing. Not a default and not a coin flip. The job stays disputed and can be put to the network again. A default would be a decision nobody made, taken with somebody else's money.

The complaint is not evidence. The prompt says so: it is the client's account, and if it describes a fault that is not actually in the delivery, that alone is reason to release. Otherwise a client could win by complaining loudly.

This runs on Studionet, deliberately. On testnet-asimov emit_transfer records a payout and leaves balances untouched, measured across two deployments. A contract whose whole point is that money moves cannot demonstrate it there.
